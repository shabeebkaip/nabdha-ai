The Market Problem

AI supply is exploding. Buying it responsibly is not getting easier.

Companies know they need AI. What they lack is a safe, structured way to find the right provider — and providers lack a credible channel to reach demand that is actually ready to buy.

Companies can't evaluate AI vendors

No shared vocabulary, no comparable pricing, no way to verify a provider's real track record before committing budget.

Providers chase unqualified leads

Time is spent on proposals for buyers who were never ready — no visibility into a customer's seriousness or payment history.

No neutral trust layer exists

Reviews are scattered, self-reported, or absent. Neither side has a reliable signal of how the other behaves in a project.

End-to-End Marketplace Journey

From a business problem to a completed, rated project.

The full lifecycle, seen from above. Each step is expanded in its own section later in this presentation.

Step 01

Business problem

The company recognises a need — e.g. reducing time spent on customer response.

Step 02

Guided request

A structured intake form captures objective, industry, budget, timeline, and requirements.

Step 03

AI assistance

Free-text input is classified into a suggested category, solution type, and objective.

Step 04

Admin review

NEXORA reviews the structured request before release to the matching engine.

Step 05

Provider matching

Eligible providers are ranked using weighted, explainable criteria.

Step 06

Invitations

A shortlist of providers is notified and may accept, decline, or submit a proposal.

Step 07

Proposals

Providers submit scope, price, timeline, warranty, and terms.

Step 08

Comparison

The customer compares all proposals side by side on one screen.

Step 09

Provider selection

The customer selects a provider and moves the request into negotiation, then contract.

Step 10

Project execution

The project proceeds outside or alongside the platform, tracked through status updates.

Step 11

Completion

Both sides confirm delivery and the request is marked complete.

Step 12

Double-blind ratings

Customer and provider rate each other; results reveal only once both have submitted.

Customer Journey

What the company sees, does, and receives.

Step 01

Create company account

Company name, industry, size, contact person, email, mobile, password. Verified by email / SMS / OTP.

Step 02

Request an AI solution

Completes the guided intake form, optionally assisted by the AI request assistant.

Step 03

Receive Request ID

System generates an ID (e.g. NEX-REQ-000125); dashboard shows status Under Review.

Step 04

Get matched & notified

Notification: "Your request has been received." Status advances to Matched.

Step 05

Review proposals

Notification: "A new proposal has been received." Proposals accumulate under the request.

Step 06

Compare & message

Uses the comparison view and internal messaging to negotiate directly with shortlisted providers.

Step 07

Select a provider

Notification: "You have been selected to execute the project" mirrors on the provider side.

Step 08

Track the project

Follows status through Contracted → In Progress → Completed from the dashboard.

Step 09

Rate the experience

Rates the provider, then separately rates NEXORA on ease of use, matching quality, and support.

08 / 29

Provider Journey

From registration to a rated, repeatable revenue channel.

Step 01

Register as a provider

Company name, logo, description, location, services, specialisations, industries served, technologies, experience, past projects, pricing range, delivery time, team, portfolio links.

Step 02

Pending verification

Account status is Pending Verification until reviewed by NEXORA administration.

Step 03

Profile goes live

Once approved, a shareable public profile is published, marked Verified by NEXORA.

Step 04

Receive request invitations

Notification: "You have a new request that matches your services."

Step 05

Accept, decline, or propose

Responds with a full proposal — solution, price, timeline, scope, support, warranty, payment terms.

Step 06

Negotiate

Uses internal messaging to clarify scope and terms directly with the customer.

Step 07

Get selected

Notification of selection; project status moves to Contracted → In Progress.

Step 08

Deliver & complete

Tracks active and completed projects, customers, and revenue from the provider dashboard.

Step 09

Rate the customer

Rates the customer's clarity, cooperation, and payment commitment — building the Client Reputation Score.

Discovery Model

Two ways to find a partner — not just one.

The original flow assumes every customer needs a guided request. Many already know exactly what they want and should be able to browse directly.

Guided / Managed MVP

Submit a request → NEXORA matches & invites providers → compare proposals. Best for buyers who don't yet know what solution they need.

Self-Serve Discovery Optional enhancement

Browse verified providers by category, industry, technology, or rating — contact directly, or invite one to an existing request. Best for buyers who already know the solution type.

Provider apply-to-request Future phase

Beyond invitation-only matching, a customer could optionally mark a request as Open to Applications — letting interested providers apply directly, alongside NEXORA's matched shortlist.

Backend / API — the shared engine

One database, one set of business rules. Not a fourth product priced separately — it's the foundation the Marketplace, the Admin Dashboard, and (if merged) the Website all run on.

What this means for scope

  • ✓1 Marketplace web app — buyer and provider panels combined, role-based, not two separate builds
  • ✓1 Admin Dashboard — internal tool, architecturally separate from the marketplace
  • ✓1 Backend / API — shared foundation, priced once, not per product
  • ✓1 Marketing website — open decision: built inside the Marketplace app, or as a fully separate site
  • ✓Native mobile app — explicitly future-phase (see Roadmap), not counted in this breakdown
Website inside the Marketplace app is faster to build and easier to maintain (one codebase, one deployment). A fully separate website gives more flexibility for marketing/SEO and can use lighter, cheaper hosting — but means two codebases to keep in sync. Integrity boundary — needs NEXORA sign-off: paid placement should only affect visibility in browse/search results — never the objective weighted score used to select and rank providers for an actual customer request. Technical Ownership & Handover

Nothing about NEXORA is locked to its development vendor.

A contractual requirement, not a courtesy: once project fees are settled, every NEXORA-specific asset is handed over in full.

Delivered to NEXORA

  • ✓Full source code & repository access
  • ✓Database & its schema
  • ✓UI/UX & design files
  • ✓Technical, API & deployment documentation
  • ✓Server / cloud account access

Guarantee

The platform must be transferable to another development company at any time — no proprietary lock-in, no withheld credentials, no undocumented dependencies.

Key Decisions Needed From NEXORA

Not just product. Platform, market, and operations — before technical work begins.

Cross-checked against a market study of Fiverr, Upwork, Toptal, Clutch, GoodFirms, and Catalant/BTG (see the companion Discovery Questionnaire) so every item below is genuinely open — nothing here duplicates a recommendation already made elsewhere in this deck.

Platform & Matching
  • ●Confirm the Core MVP scope shown on the roadmap
  • ●Confirm illustrative matching weights or supply preferred criteria, and the typical number of providers invited per request
  • ●Decide whether self-serve provider browse & search ships in MVP or later
  • ●Decide whether requests can be Open to Applications, or invite-only only
  • ●For high-value requests, decide whether NEXORA staff should hand-pick a shortlist rather than rely purely on the algorithm
  • ●Confirm the guest experience: browse and start a request anonymously, sign up only to submit (see §05 Guest Experience)
Verification & Trust
  • ●Confirm tiered verification badges (Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum) vs a single "Verified" flag
  • ●Decide whether verification includes financial/legal checks (commercial registration, credit standing) or portfolio/references only
  • ●Decide whether vetting is selective or open-with-review, and whether providers are re-verified periodically
Payments & Monetization
  • ●Decide whether online payments are in-scope for MVP or deferred
  • ●Confirm target commission percentage, which side(s) pay it, and whether there's a minimum project size or commission floor
  • ●Confirm whether provider subscription / promoted placement is in scope for any phase, and approve the "visibility-only, never matching-score" boundary
  • ●Decide lead-credit vs flat-subscription monetization model for providers
Categories, Market & Identity
  • ●Confirm or edit the initial AI service category list, and whether categories stay NEXORA-managed only or open to suggestions
  • ●Decide KSA-only launch vs GCC-wide from day one
  • ●Provide the official NEXORA visual identity for design application, and confirm "NEXORA" is a cleared, registered brand name in KSA
  • ●Decide whether the marketing website lives inside the Marketplace app or as a fully separate site (see Product Breakdown, §19)
  • ●Select preferred SMS / email / AI service providers, if any are mandated
  • ●Approve the recommended proposal & project status sets
Go-To-Market & Supply
  • ●Decide how NEXORA recruits its first cohort of verified providers before public launch (outbound, referral incentives, or open signup), and whether there's a flagship-provider target per category
  • ●Decide how NEXORA acquires its first customers, and whether launch is supply-first, demand-first, or simultaneous
Team & Operations
  • ●Confirm who staffs the NEXORA admin team at launch, and in what roles
  • ●Set target SLAs for provider verification and request-review turnaround
  • ●Confirm the customer/provider support model (email, live chat, or full helpdesk) and expected response time
  • ●Confirm NEXORA's day-to-day technical point of contact for the build
Legal, Compliance & Risk
  • ●Confirm applicable KSA marketplace/commercial regulations, and how VAT is handled on commission revenue
  • ●Confirm the data protection posture relative to Saudi PDPL, given the PII and financial data the platform stores
  • ●Decide whether NEXORA provides a standard contract/SOW template between customer and provider
  • ●Decide what recourse, if any, NEXORA offers if a provider abandons a project or a customer refuses to pay — since payment stays off-platform at MVP
Budget & Success Metrics
  • ●Confirm whether the financial proposal should target a fixed budget ceiling, or be scoped from requirements first
  • ●Define target metrics for the first 3 / 6 / 12 months — providers onboarded, requests submitted, projects completed, transaction value
  • ●Confirm who owns continued iteration after MVP launch — an ongoing retainer with the development team, or an in-house NEXORA team
